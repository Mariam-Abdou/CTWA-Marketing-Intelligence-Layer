## Marketing Intelligence Layer - 6AM Cohort

> **Finish line - Aug 28, 2026:** Ship a continuous-learning system that turns WhatsApp conversation + Meta ad data into a scoreboard + next-campaign recommendation at three levels (campaign, adset, ad). You're judged on honesty on data you never see, not a leaderboard score.

### What You Hand In

One row per ID with a calibrated score and an action a human can take. The exact schema is yours to design; for example:

```json
{
  "level": "ad",
  "id": "120209017",
  "score": 0.78,
  "action": "scale",
  "bucket": "exploit",
  "why": [
    "3 sales / 18 clicks",
    "CTR stable over 14 days"
  ]
}
```[cite: 1]

Same shape keyed at `campaign_id` and `adset_id`.[cite: 1] Plus:
* **Code:** The code that regenerates the table from the data (re-runnable, config-driven, no notebook archaeology).[cite: 1]
* **Evaluation:** An honest held-out evaluation (calibration + simulated business impact) on a holdout you reserve yourself, kept separate from the secret set we grade on, which you never see.[cite: 1]
* **Documentation:** A 1-2 page architecture note + a few merchant-readable findings.[cite: 1]

## The Problem

A premium-food merchant in Cairo runs Meta ads $\rightarrow$ customers land in WhatsApp $\rightarrow$ they chat, buy, refund, come back.[cite: 1] Meta only ever sees the click.[cite: 1] Every message, order, and refund after it is first-party data that today sits unused in the chat logs.[cite: 1] Your job: turn it into campaign decisions.[cite: 1]

### The Three Levels Your Recommendation Answers
1. **Campaign:** The goal (Sales / Leads / Awareness)[cite: 1]
2. **Ad Set:** The audience (+ budget)[cite: 1]
3. **Ad:** The creative (what the customer actually saw)[cite: 1]

Your scoreboard scores all three.[cite: 1] Your recommendation answers all three: which strategy, which audience, which creative.[cite: 1]

## The 70/30 Rule - Exploit AND Explore

Your recommendation splits the next budget—it doesn't just rank winners:[cite: 1]

* **~70% Exploit:** Scale what's proven, with high confidence.[cite: 1]
* **~30% Explore:** 2-3 specific tests, each with a hypothesis and a stop criterion.[cite: 1]

**Why both:** Pure "scale what works" collapses when winners fatigue or the season shifts; pure "test everything" burns budget.[cite: 1] The split moves with your confidence and the season: more explore when confidence is low or a new cycle begins (Ramadan $\rightarrow$ Eid $\rightarrow$ Summer).[cite: 1]

## Continuous Learning: One System, Static + Dynamic Parts

It is one system, not a model.[cite: 1] Some parts of it are built once and never change; one part retrains every cycle.[cite: 1] Keeping the two separate is what lets the system keep getting sharper without being rebuilt.[cite: 1]

* **Static (The skeleton, built once):** The pipeline that ingests and joins the data, the decision rules (scoring, the 70/30 split, the actions), the output schema, and how you evaluate.[cite: 1] Same every cycle.[cite: 1]
* **Dynamic (The muscle that grows):** The predictors inside the system that learn (outcome / LTV / creative-effectiveness / segmentation), re-fit each cycle on all the data so far (past cycles plus the new one) as new data arrives.[cite: 1] Their numbers move and the recommendations get sharper.[cite: 1]

**How the two fit together:** The static parts only ever see a fixed output shape from the dynamic parts: a calibrated score plus a confidence.[cite: 1] That fixed shape is the contract between them.[cite: 1] Because the shape never changes, you can retrain, tune, or swap any predictor between cycles and nothing else in the system has to change.[cite: 1] The skeleton defines the sockets; the predictors are what plug into them.[cite: 1]

> *Skeleton stays; the muscles get stronger every cycle.*[cite: 1]

## Non-Negotiable Rules

* **Holdout is sacred:** Touch it once, at the very end.[cite: 1] Tuning on it invalidates the result.[cite: 1]
* **Never random-split:** Split by `customer.id` or by time window; repeat customers leak across train/test.[cite: 1]
* **Opaque IDs:** `ctwa_clid`, `source_url`, and `conv.id` are labels, not features.[cite: 1]
* **Calibration matters:** "70% confidence" must be right ~70% of the time.[cite: 1]
* **Numbers are upper bounds:** Small teaching data; >0.85 AUC on message text alone means you overfit templates.[cite: 1]

---

## How We Grade - Honesty, Not a High Score

When you submit, we run your system on a fresh set of conversations you never saw, and judge six things:[cite: 1]

1. **Reproducible:** We re-run it and get the numbers you reported (it runs from the data, no undocumented hand-tweaks).[cite: 1]
2. **Honest:** No test data leaked into training.[cite: 1] Split by customer or by time, never randomly or the same customer lands on both sides and the score is fake.[cite: 1]
3. **Calibrated:** When you say "82% likely to buy," it happens ~82% of the time.[cite: 1] Confidence has to mean something.[cite: 1]
4. **Useful to a merchant:** The output drives a real decision ("prioritise these chats", "kill this ad"), not just a metric or a model file.[cite: 1]
5. **Clear about limits:** You state what the model does not know (one merchant, 180 days, no inventory/footfall upper bounds, not promises).[cite: 1]
6. **Extendable:** Someone can read your write-up and add a signal or swap a model without a rewrite.[cite: 1]

> *The data is small and messy on purpose. A shiny 0.95 almost always means you fooled yourself. We reward honesty, calibration, and clear limits over a high score.*[cite: 1]
